QUESTION 2 - REFLECTION
MindBridge: QA Strategy and Execution Document
Dennis | 07 August 2026

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In 3-5 sentences, describe one decision you made during the lab that improved
the quality of your QA Strategy document. Explain what you decided, why you
made that choice, and what a weaker version of that decision would have looked
like.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The decision that most improved the document was to run a static ambiguity
review of story MB-142 before writing a single test case, rather than treating
the seeded story as a finished specification. The story said only that the app
"should give them a link", which left link expiry, viewer permissions,
revocation and repeat-request behaviour entirely undefined, so I walked it
through with the product owner and dev lead and converted each gap into an
explicit, testable acceptance criterion. That choice is what allowed Section 3
to contain a genuine edge case and a genuine error condition instead of three
variations on the happy path, and Scenario 2 - a second share request for a
dashboard that already holds an active link - is precisely the path that
exposed the HTTP 500 defect logged as MB-DEF-001 in Section 4. A weaker version
of the same decision would have been to accept the story as written and begin
executing straight away, which would have produced one happy-path check that
passes, an empty defect log, and a false signal that the feature was ready to
ship. The review cost roughly forty minutes and moved defect discovery from
production back into the sprint, which is the entire economic argument for
static testing.
